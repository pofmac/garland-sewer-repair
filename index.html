Signs your sewer line needs attention

Drains backing up in more than one place

When the tub gurgles as the toilet flushes, or a floor drain overflows, the blockage is usually in the main sewer line, not a single drain.

Soggy, green or sunken patches in the yard

A broken line leaks wastewater into the soil. Grass over the line grows greener, or the ground sinks along the pipe's path.

Sewer smell indoors or outside

A steady sewage odor can mean a crack or a gap in the pipe, or a dry trap letting sewer gas in.

Clogs that keep coming back

If a main-line clog returns weeks after it was cleared, tree roots or a sagging section of pipe are often the cause.

Why sewer lines fail in Garland

Garland's clay soil

Much of the Dallas area sits on expansive clay that swells when wet and shrinks in drought. That movement bends and cracks buried pipes, and it is worst after a long, hot, dry summer.

Older pipes

Many Garland homes were built from the 1950s through the 1980s. Depending on the year, the sewer line may be cast iron, clay tile or even Orangeburg (tar paper pipe), all of which wear out with age.

Tree roots

Roots grow toward the moisture in sewer lines and push in through small joints and cracks, then grow until they block the pipe.
